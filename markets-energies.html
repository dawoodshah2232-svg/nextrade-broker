Trade Energies

Trade Brent and WTI crude oil and natural gas. Energy prices react to supply, OPEC decisions, demand and geopolitics, and they can move sharply on news.

Brent, WTI and Natural Gas

Brent crude is the benchmark for oil from the North Sea and the reference price for much of the world’s oil. WTI (West Texas Intermediate) is the benchmark for US crude. Natural gas is the fuel used for heating and power generation, and its price is driven by weather, storage levels and demand. You trade CFDs on their prices, without handling any physical oil or gas.

What Moves Oil Prices?

Oil prices move on supply, demand, OPEC decisions and geopolitics. Production cuts or increases by OPEC and other large producers, changes in global demand, weekly inventory reports, and conflict or disruption in producing regions can all move prices sharply, sometimes within minutes of an announcement. Natural gas is also sensitive to weather: a cold winter raises heating demand and can push prices up quickly.

Trading Hours

Brent, WTI and natural gas trade almost around the clock on weekdays, with a short daily break. Liquidity is usually highest during the London and New York sessions, and spreads can widen around inventory reports and OPEC announcements.

Risks to Know

Energy prices can gap sharply on news, and leverage of up to 1:500 magnifies both gains and losses. A position held over a weekend is exposed to Monday’s opening price, which can differ from Friday’s close after a weekend event. Never trade with money you cannot afford to lose, and read the Risk Disclosure before you trade.
