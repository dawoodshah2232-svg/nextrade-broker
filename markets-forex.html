Trade Forex

Trade the world’s largest market. Forex is the buying and selling of currencies in pairs, and it trades around the clock on weekdays, with deep liquidity and tight spreads.

What Is Forex Trading?

Forex, or foreign exchange, is the market where currencies are exchanged. Every trade involves two currencies quoted as a pair, such as EURUSD. The first currency is the base currency and the second is the quote currency. When you buy EURUSD, you are buying euros and selling US dollars; when you sell, you are doing the opposite. You trade on whether you expect the pair to rise or fall, without ever handling the currencies themselves.

The forex market is the largest financial market in the world, open around the clock on weekdays. Because so many banks, companies and traders take part, major pairs are highly liquid, which keeps typical spreads tight.

Major, Minor and Exotic Pairs

Major pairs

Pairs that include the US dollar and another leading currency: EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY, AUDUSD and USDCHF. They are the most traded pairs and usually carry the tightest spreads.

Minor pairs

Pairs of leading currencies that do not include the US dollar, such as EURGBP, EURJPY and GBPJPY. They are liquid but usually have slightly wider spreads than the majors.

Exotic pairs

Pairs that combine a leading currency with a currency from a smaller or emerging economy, such as USDTRY or USDZAR. They can move sharply and usually carry wider spreads.

Trading Hours

Forex trades from Sunday 22:00 GMT to Friday 22:00 GMT. The market follows the business day around the world: Sydney and Tokyo open first, then London, then New York. Liquidity and volatility are usually highest during the London and New York sessions, and quietest during the Asian session and around weekends.

What Moves Currencies?

Currency prices move on interest rates, economic data and central bank decisions. When a central bank raises rates, its currency often strengthens; when it cuts rates, it often weakens. Employment reports, inflation figures and growth data can also move prices sharply, as can political events and shifts in market sentiment.

Risks to Know

Forex trading on leverage can produce losses larger than your deposit where negative balance protection does not apply. Prices can gap over news releases, spreads can widen suddenly, and even major pairs can move quickly. Never trade with money you cannot afford to lose, and read the Risk Disclosure before you trade.
