Trade Indices

Trade whole markets in a single position. Index CFDs let you trade on the performance of leading stock markets without buying any individual share.

What Is an Index?

An index groups a basket of shares together to measure the performance of a market or a sector. Instead of choosing one company, you trade on the value of the basket as a whole. The US30 follows 30 large US companies, the NAS100 follows 100 large non-financial companies listed on the Nasdaq, and the S&P 500 follows 500 large US companies. Indices are popular with traders who want broad exposure to a market rather than a view on one company.

What Moves Indices?

Indices move on company earnings, economic data, interest rate decisions and the broader mood of the market. A strong earnings season can lift an index, while weak economic data or expectations of higher rates can pull it down. Because an index holds many companies, the news of any single company matters less than it would for that company’s share alone.

Trading Hours

Index trading hours follow the underlying exchanges, so each index trades mainly while its home exchange is open, with some indices also available outside those hours. Spreads can widen outside the main session, so check the hours for each index on the platform before you trade.

Risks to Know

Indices can fall as fast as they rise, and leverage of up to 1:500 magnifies both gains and losses. Prices can gap after market-moving news, and a position held over a weekend is exposed to Monday’s opening price. Never trade with money you cannot afford to lose, and read the Risk Disclosure before you trade.
