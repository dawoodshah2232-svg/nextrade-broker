What Is a Share CFD?

A share CFD is a contract that tracks the price of a listed company’s shares. When you trade it, you gain exposure to the price movement of the shares without owning them. You can open a long position if you expect the price to rise, or a short position if you expect it to fall. Because you do not own the shares, you have no voting rights, and you do not receive dividends in the same way a shareholder does: instead, dividend adjustments may be applied to your position as a cash adjustment, where applicable.

Typical Spreads

Typical spreads vary by share. Each company’s shares have a different price and a different level of liquidity, so there is no single spread that applies to all shares. Check the spread for the specific share on the trading platform before you place an order. Spreads vary with market conditions and can widen around company news and at times of low liquidity.

What Moves Share Prices?

Share prices move on company earnings, product news, management changes, sector trends and the broader market. A strong earnings report can lift a share, while a profit warning can send it down sharply in a single session. Interest rate decisions also matter: higher rates tend to weigh on growth shares in particular.

Trading Hours

Share CFD trading hours follow the exchanges where the shares are listed, so each share trades mainly during the hours of its home exchange. Spreads can widen outside the main session, so check the hours for each share on the platform before you trade.

Risks to Know

A single share can fall sharply on company news, and leverage of up to 1:500 magnifies both gains and losses. A position held overnight is exposed to the next day’s opening price, which can gap after earnings released outside trading hours. Never trade with money you cannot afford to lose, and read the Risk Disclosure before you trade.
