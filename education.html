Trading basics

What a pip is

A pip is the smallest standard price move in most currency pairs — usually the fourth decimal place. For example, if EUR/USD moves from 1.1000 to 1.1001, that is a move of one pip. Pips are how traders measure price movement and, together with position size, how gains and losses are counted.

Lots and position size

A lot is a standard unit of trade size. A standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency, a mini lot is 10,000 units, and a micro lot is 1,000 units. Position size decides how much each pip is worth to you — larger positions mean larger gains and larger losses for the same price move.

Spread

The spread is the difference between the buy price (ask) and the sell price (bid) of an instrument. You buy at the ask and sell at the bid, so the spread is a cost of opening a trade. Spreads shown on this site are typical spreads from the stated level and vary with market conditions.

Leverage

Leverage is the ratio between the size of your position and the margin required to open it. With leverage up to 1:500, a smaller margin can control a larger position. Leverage increases both the size of potential gains and the size of potential losses, and it can lead to losses greater than your deposit where negative balance protection does not apply.

Glossary

Twelve terms every trader should know.

Pip

The smallest standard price move in most currency pairs, usually the fourth decimal place.

Lot

A standard unit of trade size — a standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency.

Spread

The difference between the buy price (ask) and the sell price (bid) of an instrument.

Leverage

The ratio between position size and the margin required to open it; it magnifies both gains and losses.

Margin

The deposit required to open and hold a leveraged position.

Margin Call

A warning that your account equity has fallen close to the margin required to keep positions open.

Stop Loss

An order that closes a position automatically at a set price to limit the loss on that trade.

Take Profit

An order that closes a position automatically at a set price to lock in a gain on that trade.

Swap

The interest adjustment applied when a position is held open overnight.

CFD

A contract for difference — an agreement to exchange the difference in an asset's price, without owning the asset itself.

Bid/Ask

The bid is the price at which you can sell; the ask is the price at which you can buy.

Equity

Your account balance plus or minus the open profit or loss on your positions.
